# Environmental recurrence selects a costly memory-use policy across two operator bundles without a resolved one-bit population benefit

**Jack Chen**

Frederick Sequencing and Genomics Core; Advanced Biomedical Computational Science (ABCS); Frederick National Lab for Cancer Research; National Institutes of Health

Research preprint draft, version 1.2.0; not externally peer reviewed

## Abstract

Prior theory and experiments establish that environmental predictability can favor memory-like inheritance, learning and anticipatory effects, and that memory can carry costs. We asked narrower, prospectively specified questions in an abstract computational model, in three prospective studies spanning two operator bundles. Does lag-two environmental recurrence enrich a mutable allele for using a supplied one-record private cache when each retrieval displaces one of two fresh exploratory proposals; does the directional information in the retrieved record, rather than the mere loss of a fresh proposal, drive that selection; does either coincide with a meaningful gain in population accuracy; and does the resulting separation of selection from performance recur in an independently implemented alternative operator bundle? In a finite population of 32 haploid searchers, a binary use policy mutated symmetrically at rate 1/32 and was selected only through the objective scores of the candidates it caused to be proposed. Label-blind SHAM arms supplied an exact neutral benchmark for the label process. In Study 001, across 41,600 independent paired blocks (332,800 paths and 10,905,190,400 objective queries), late-window start contrasts were bounded within one expected individual (95% familywise) over the 256-update horizon, passing the prespecified start-state gate, and recurrence strongly enriched the memory-use allele. Relative to the neutral 1/2, the start-averaged excess was 0.310292 (95% familywise Hoeffding-Bonferroni interval [0.302482, 0.318102]); relative to the nonrecurrent law it was 0.441635 ([0.426015, 0.457256]). By contrast, the recurrent-law ACTIVE minus SHAM accuracy contrast was 0.009377 ([-0.005138, 0.023892]), bounded below the prespecified one-correct-bit scale of 0.03125, with sign unresolved, and the recurrence interaction in accuracy, 0.011360 ([-0.017669, 0.040390]), was unresolved. Under the nonrecurrent law, ACTIVE accuracy was descriptively 0.001983 below SHAM. Study 002, a post-v1.0 prospective extension with a new cohort of 41,600 blocks (249,600 paths and 8,178,892,800 objective queries), kept only the aligned recurrent law and compared directional cache retrieval (INFO) with a recurrence-gated control that preserves each retrieved candidate's distance to its parent but scrambles its direction (NONINFO). Both start contrasts again passed the gate. Directional information raised late-window memory-use frequency by 0.184203 ([0.169688, 0.198718]) relative to NONINFO, which itself exceeded the neutral 1/2 by 0.126083 ([0.118825, 0.133340]). The frozen primary estimand, the total population-accuracy effect of directional information relative to NONINFO, was 0.008650 ([-0.006521, 0.023821]): bounded below the positive one-bit scale, wholly inside ±1/32 and with sign unresolved. Under one deliberately aligned recurrence law and one fixed opportunity cost, directional cache information therefore strongly amplified selection on an information-use policy, while its total effect on population performance remained bounded below one correct bit. Study 003, a post-v1.1 prospective extension, asked the Study 001 question again in one independently implemented alternative operator bundle: 32 circular loci with 2^16 alleles each, graded circular loss, uniform fresh proposals and four-entry rank-tournament survival, with entirely new code, Threefry random addressing and cohorts (41,600 blocks, 332,800 paths and 10,905,190,400 objective queries). Both start contrasts lay wholly inside ±1/32, and recurrence again enriched the memory-use allele, by 0.282878 ([0.275067, 0.290688]) relative to the neutral 1/2 and by 0.424421 ([0.408801, 0.440042]) relative to the nonrecurrent law, so the frozen primary decision supports recurrence-attributable selective enrichment in this alternative operator bundle. Its recurrent-law ACTIVE minus SHAM performance contrast, 0.005057 ([-0.009458, 0.019571]), was bounded below the positive 1/32 scale with sign unresolved, and the recurrence interaction, 0.006286 ([-0.022743, 0.035316]), was unresolved: the selection/performance separation recurred in this operator bundle. The results do not establish universal replication, cannot attribute the Study 003 outcome to any single operator, and do not address the origin of memory architecture, other recurrence laws, other costs or biological systems.

## 1. Introduction

Recurring environments make the past informative about the future, and a substantial literature shows that this can favor carrying information across time. Kuijper and Johnstone found that environmental stability and predictability can favor positive parental effects and long-term phenotypic memory under genetically determined transmission rules [1]. Carja and Plotkin showed that, in periodically fluctuating environments, an intermediate degree of phenotypic memory maximizes the fixation probability of an allele that permits heritable phenotypic heterogeneity [2]. Dey, Proulx and Teotónio showed experimentally that temporal environmental structure can select maternal effects [8]; Skanata and Kussell derived evolutionary regimes for response networks with phenotypic memory in random environments [9]; and Kronholm found that anticipatory effects mediated by epigenetic changes evolve when an environmental cue is reliable, the environment changes at intermediate rates and the costs of the effect are low [7].

Models of learning reach related conclusions. Wright and colleagues allowed memory weighting and current sampling effort to evolve under explicit costs and found that memory increased with temporal autocorrelation and interacted with costly sampling [3]. Arehart and Adler quantified the costs and benefits of memory duration and exploration in a minimal learning model and found that the marginal benefit of learning is often small [4]. Morgan, Suchow and Griffiths inserted human participants as agents into evolutionary simulations with simulated loci for learning and memory and found that rapid environmental change dampened their evolution [5]. Among the studies identified by our bounded search, this is a close precedent for an evolving memory-use trait, although its learners were human participants rather than a cleanly mutable allele. Other work is at least as close on other dimensions: Carja and Plotkin analyzed a mutable modifier allele and its fixation in a finite population [2], and Grabowski and colleagues studied the early evolution of memory usage in digital organisms [6].

The value and cost of memory that is already present are also well studied. Dunlap and Stephens linked environmental change to optimal sampling and memory weighting [10]. Lambert and Kussell measured bacterial memory under fluctuating carbon sources and modeled its long-term fitness benefit [11]. Rescan and colleagues linked phenotypic memory to population growth and extinction risk in a noisy environment [12]. George optimized memory-driven phenotypic adaptation in fluctuating environments [13], and Jain, Jolly and George added adaptation costs [14]. Pollack, Nozoe and Kussell analyzed selection on gene regulation and phenotypic memory under metabolic fluctuations [15]. Leadbeater and Watrobska emphasize that memory can be shaped by selection along many mechanistic dimensions rather than as a single scalar trait [16].

Against this background, we do not claim to show that memory evolves, that predictability favors memory, or that costly memory can be selected; those propositions are occupied by the work above. The contribution here is a specific joint design, executed with a new implementation and new random cohorts, that allows two outcomes to be judged separately. First, the memory architecture, a one-record private cache, is supplied, and only a binary use policy mutates and is selected, in a finite population with explicitly stochastic survival. Second, the cost of using memory is a one-for-one search-budget opportunity cost: a carrier that retrieves its cache gives up one of its two fresh proposals, with no added fitness penalty or tunable coefficient. Third, a label-blind SHAM arm supplies an exact neutral benchmark for the label process at the same query budget. Fourth, allele enrichment and population accuracy were judged by prospectively separate decision rules, each with a stated meaningful scale. Fifth, a recurrence-gated, parent-distance-matched control separates the directional content of a retrieved record from the loss of the fresh proposal it displaces. Sixth, the central Study 001 classification was tested again, prospectively, in an independently implemented operator bundle with a different alphabet, loss, proposal rule and survival rule and with entirely new cohorts.

The separation matters because the two outcomes need not move together. An allele can rise in frequency because it improves its carriers' chances of being among the survivors while the population's average fit to the environment changes little. Carriers may displace non-carriers that would have achieved similar accuracy, or gains at recurrent updates may be offset by lost exploration at non-recurrent updates. Treating allele enrichment as evidence of population benefit would conflate these possibilities.

We report the accepted results of three prospective studies in two operator bundles. In Study 001 (PHASE2-MUTABLE-MEMORY-001), under one recurrent target law that was deliberately aligned with the cache delay, lag-two recurrence strongly enriched the mutation-generated memory-use allele above both its exact neutral expectation and its frequency under a nonrecurrent control, despite the fixed opportunity cost. The corresponding ACTIVE minus SHAM accuracy contrast was bounded below the prespecified one-correct-bit scale, with sign unresolved, and the recurrence interaction in accuracy remained unresolved. Study 001 could not say how much of that selection responded to what the cache contained rather than to the fresh proposal that retrieval displaced. Study 002 (PHASE2-PERFORMANCE-CONVERSION-002), a post-v1.0 prospective extension with a new random cohort, addressed that question within the aligned law by comparing directional retrieval (INFO) with a recurrence-gated control that keeps each retrieved candidate at the cache's distance from its parent but scrambles its direction (NONINFO). Directional information strongly increased selection on the memory-use policy, yet its total effect on population accuracy was bounded below the one-bit scale with sign unresolved. Studies 001 and 002 share one operator bundle, so neither could say whether the separation depends on its binary alphabet, Hamming loss, XOR-mask proposals and weighted survival. Study 003 (PHASE2-TORUS-MEMORY-003), a post-v1.1 prospective extension, re-tested the Study 001 primary classification in one alternative bundle, a 32-locus torus with 2^16 alleles per locus, graded circular loss, uniform fresh proposals and four-entry rank-tournament survival, implemented anew with new random addressing and cohorts. Recurrence-attributable enrichment recurred, and the recurrent-law performance contrast was again bounded below the positive 1/32 scale with sign unresolved. Because alphabet, loss, resampling and survival changed together, Study 003 cannot attribute this outcome to any one of them. We present the three studies as a single result: in the first operator bundle, recurrence selected a costly memory-use policy, directional cache information strongly amplified that selection, and neither was accompanied by a demonstrated commensurate population-performance advantage at the fixed scale; in one independently implemented alternative bundle, recurrence-attributable enrichment and a bounded performance contrast recurred together.

## 2. Model and prospective design

This paper reports three prospective studies in two operator bundles. Sections 2.1-2.7 describe Study 001, Section 2.8 describes the Study 002 extension, which changes only the construction of one candidate, and Section 2.9 describes Study 003, which poses the Study 001 question again in an independently implemented alternative operator bundle. For each study, the specification, decision table, cohort size, audit plan and claim limits were frozen before any production outcome was generated. For Study 001, a non-implementing AI reviewer returned a terminal GO review of the frozen specification before production was authorized; the specification hash, review record and timestamps are given in Section 7. In no study was any parameter, cohort, horizon, target law, mutation rate or cost changed after outcomes were observed. The complete update order is given in Supplementary Sections S2-S7 for Study 001, S17-S18 for Study 002 and S28-S31 for Study 003.

### 2.1 Population, search state and objective

The population contains 32 haploid individuals in fixed slots 0-31. Each individual carries an unsigned 32-bit genotype and a binary policy allele, `M` (use the private cache) or `F` (fresh exploration). An `M` individual also holds a private cache that is either invalid or contains one 32-bit genotype; `F` individuals hold no valid cache. The allele jointly determines whether the one-step record is retained and whether it is retrieved; every individual has a cache slot, but only `M` carriers receive a valid record, so this design cannot separate selection on retention from selection on retrieval. Lineage identifiers are audit metadata only and are never read by any scientific operator. Each path runs for updates 1-256. At update t the environment presents a 32-bit target `T_t`, and the raw objective of a genotype is its Hamming mismatch to `T_t`. Population accuracy at an update is one minus the mean survivor mismatch divided by 32, measured after survival; a population of uniformly random genotypes has expected accuracy 1/2. The late window is updates 193-256 inclusive.

The population size, bit length, four-candidate architecture, lag-two law and selection weights were inherited from earlier internal private-memory studies so that the changed question, selection on a mutable use policy, would be identifiable. The implementation and all random cohorts are new and import no producer code from those studies. This study is not a replication of them.

### 2.2 Candidate budget and the one-proposal opportunity cost

Every parent produces exactly four evaluated candidates per update, so every update makes exactly 128 objective queries regardless of allele, arm or law. The candidates are (1) the unchanged parent; (2) a policy probe; (3) a global scout, the parent XOR an independent uniform 32-bit mask; and (4) a local child, made by taking the lowest-mismatch member of candidates 1-3 (ties broken by a keyed random key and then by family order) and flipping each of its 32 bits independently with probability 1/32. For an `F` individual the policy probe is the parent XOR a second independent uniform mask, so an `F` carrier makes two fresh, effectively uniform proposals per update. For a valid `M` carrier in the ACTIVE arm, the policy probe is the cached genotype itself. Because the probe is also eligible to be the donor of the local child, a useful cache can shape two of the four candidates.

The cost of memory is therefore an opportunity cost on a fixed search budget: retrieval replaces exactly one of two effectively uniform fresh proposals with the cached record. There is no score penalty and no tunable cost coefficient. This is a model-specific search-budget trade-off, not a calibrated energetic, metabolic or biological cost, and its magnitude is conditional on the frozen fresh-probe distribution. When the cache is uninformative, an `M` carrier loses one fresh draw at the same query count. When the cache closely matches the current target, retrieval supplies a candidate that a uniform fresh draw is unlikely to match. Genotypes are not deduplicated: if the cache equals the unchanged parent, both copies are separately evaluated and the update still makes 128 queries.

### 2.3 Target laws and the deliberate HALF alignment

For every block and update, an innovation `I_t` (a uniform 32-bit word) and a fair recurrence bit `R_t` are generated whether or not they are used. Under ZERO, `T_t = I_t` at every update. Under HALF, `T_1 = I_1` and `T_2 = I_2`; for t >= 3 the target returns to `T_(t-2)` when `R_t = 1` and otherwise equals `I_t`. Non-recurrent HALF updates use the same innovation as ZERO at that update.

The cache of a survivor records its producing parent's genotype from the update in which the survivor was made, and that parent had itself survived selection against the previous target. A cache used at update t+1 therefore holds a genotype that survived selection against `T_(t-1)`, which under HALF is the target at update t+1 with probability 1/2. HALF was a prior, outcome-informed choice made precisely to align the one-generation cache delay with a two-update target return; it favors retrieval by construction. No other lag, copy probability, target law, cost or model class was tested.

### 2.4 Selection, policy mutation and cache inheritance

All 128 candidates are evaluated. Candidate j with mismatch `h_j` receives the exact integer weight `2^(32-h_j)`, proportional to `2^(-h_j)`. Thirty-two survivors are drawn sequentially without replacement, each with probability proportional to its weight among the candidates not yet selected. This is an exact Plackett-Luce weighted sample implemented entirely in unsigned integer arithmetic, with unbiased bounded integers produced by multiply-high rejection (Supplementary Section S5). No label, cache, lineage identifier or penalty enters selection; the policy allele affects survival only through the genotypes it causes to be proposed.

Each survivor first inherits its producing parent's policy. Then, independently in each survivor slot, the policy flips `M <-> F` with probability mu = 1/32. After mutation, an `F` survivor has an invalid cache and an `M` survivor's cache becomes its producing parent's pre-update genotype. From update 2 onward every `M` carrier therefore holds a valid one-step lineage cache. A new `F -> M` mutant first uses its record on the next update, and an `M -> F -> M` history carries no older hidden record. Allele frequencies and accuracy are recorded after this transition.

### 2.5 Paired cells and the exact SHAM benchmark

Each of 41,600 independent blocks contains the same eight paired cells: information arm (ACTIVE or SHAM) x target law (ZERO or HALF) x policy start (ALL_F or ALL_M). Within a block, all eight cells share 32 initial uniform genotypes and all coordinate-keyed random values; caches start invalid. Random values come from counter-based Philox4x32-10 streams indexed by purpose, block, update, entity and subindex, and every draw is generated whether or not it is used, so no label, branch, arm or law changes any later random value (Supplementary Section S7).

In SHAM, the policy probe is always the fresh construction, so genotypes never depend on a label or cache. Two exact identities follow. N1: SHAM genotype, mismatch, accuracy and survivor trajectories are bit-identical between the ALL_F and ALL_M starts. N2: SHAM labels in the two starts are exact complements after every update, so their memory-use frequencies sum to 1 at every update and their start-averaged late-window frequency is exactly 1/2 in every block. With symmetric mutation and r = 1 - 2mu = 15/16, the SHAM expectations are `P(M at t | ALL_F) = [1 - r^t]/2` and `P(M at t | ALL_M) = [1 + r^t]/2`. The expected ALL_M minus ALL_F difference is r^256 = 6.678005283720385e-08 at update 256 and r^193(1 - r^64)/(64(1 - r)) = 9.57983820600146e-07 averaged over the late window.

SHAM is an exact neutral benchmark for the label process (N1, N2; expected start-averaged frequency 1/2) and a paired control for query budget and keyed random draws. Because ACTIVE genotypes and genealogies diverge from SHAM at the first valid-M retrieval, SHAM does not hold realised drift or genotype state fixed for ACTIVE paths; ACTIVE-SHAM contrasts are arm-level comparisons within the model, not path-level counterfactuals. It does not by itself separate the information carried by the cache from the mere loss of one fresh proposal; the ZERO law, under which the cache carries no lag-two information, supplies that contrast through `C_rec`, and Study 002 (Section 2.8) supplies a within-law contrast. It does not represent any alternative cost, retrieval rule or memory architecture. Its neutral formulas justify the late window in expectation for SHAM only and do not establish that ACTIVE paths have converged.

### 2.6 Estimands, intervals and frozen decisions

For each block, let `L(arm, law, start)` be the mean memory-use frequency over updates 193-256. Define `A_HALF = [L(ACTIVE, HALF, ALL_F) + L(ACTIVE, HALF, ALL_M)]/2` and `A_ZERO` analogously. The four primary estimands are:

- `C_abs = A_HALF - 1/2`, the recurrent-law excess over the exact neutral expectation;
- `C_rec = A_HALF - A_ZERO`, the excess attributable to recurrence;
- `D_HALF = L(ACTIVE, HALF, ALL_M) - L(ACTIVE, HALF, ALL_F)`, the start-state contrast under HALF; and
- `D_ZERO = L(ACTIVE, ZERO, ALL_M) - L(ACTIVE, ZERO, ALL_F)`, the start-state contrast under ZERO.

Let `P(arm, law)` be start-averaged late-window mean population accuracy. The two secondary estimands are:

- `P_abs = P(ACTIVE, HALF) - P(SHAM, HALF)`, the recurrent-law accuracy contrast; and
- `P_rec = [P(ACTIVE, HALF) - P(SHAM, HALF)] - [P(ACTIVE, ZERO) - P(SHAM, ZERO)]`, the recurrence interaction in accuracy.

The meaningful allele scale is Delta = 1/32 = 0.03125, one expected individual in the 32-member population; it refers to a late-window expected excess, not to the persistence of one named carrier. The meaningful performance scale is Delta_P = 1/32, one additional correct bit per survivor on average, which is 3.125 percentage points of accuracy.

Each family is controlled at 95% familywise coverage by two-sided Hoeffding intervals with a Bonferroni split. For a block variable with range length R and n = 41,600 independent blocks, the half-width is `h = R sqrt[ln(2/alpha_each)/(2n)]`. In the primary family, alpha_each = 0.0125; `C_abs` has range length 1 and half-width 0.00781023, and `C_rec`, `D_HALF` and `D_ZERO` have range length 2 and half-width 0.01562046, just below Delta/2 = 0.015625. In the secondary family, alpha_each = 0.025; `P_abs` has range length 2 and half-width 0.01451463, and `P_rec` has range length 4 and half-width 0.02902925. No empirical variance, normal approximation, within-path independence, pilot or favorable pairing enters these bounds. The two families are controlled separately, not jointly.

The primary decision table was applied in order: (1) failure of any deterministic identity, seed or counter audit, saved-output reconstruction or query count gives INVALID; (2) if either `D_HALF` or `D_ZERO` has an interval not wholly inside (-Delta, +Delta), the result is START-DEPENDENT and the question unresolved; (3) if the lower bounds of both `C_abs` and `C_rec` exceed Delta, the result SUPPORTS RECURRENCE-ATTRIBUTABLE SELECTIVE ENRICHMENT; (4) if the lower bound of `C_abs` exceeds Delta and the upper bound of `C_rec` is at or below Delta, enrichment is not attributable to recurrence at the fixed scale; (5) if the upper bound of `C_abs` is at or below Delta, the result is a bounded negative at the one-individual scale; (6) every other pattern is unresolved. Each secondary estimand was classified, in order, as meaningful positive performance if its lower bound exceeds Delta_P, meaningful adverse performance if its upper bound is below -Delta_P, BOUNDED BELOW THE POSITIVE ONE-BIT SCALE if its upper bound is at or below Delta_P, and UNRESOLVED otherwise. Secondary classifications cannot alter the primary decision.

Sixteen absolute cell means, the late-window memory-use frequency and accuracy for each of the eight cells, were saved as descriptive records without inferential bounds. Together with the six inferential records they form the 22 prespecified saved estimate records. The descriptive means support interpretation only.

### 2.7 The start-state gate, and why allele frequency is not accuracy

The start-state gate, rule 2, asks whether populations started entirely from `F` and entirely from `M` reach the same late-window ACTIVE frequency to within one expected individual. If they did not, the late window would partly reflect where paths began rather than a late-window state approached from both starts within the frozen horizon, and no enrichment classification would be made. The gate is applied before any enrichment rule. Passing it shows only that both late-window start contrasts were bounded within (-1/32, +1/32) at 95% familywise coverage over the 256-update horizon; it does not demonstrate start-independence in general, stationarity or a unique long-run state.

Allele frequency measures which policy the survivors carry; accuracy measures how well the survivors match the target. The two are linked only through the genotypes that `M` carriers propose. A policy can raise its carriers' representation among survivors by supplying high-scoring candidates at recurrent updates while costing a fresh proposal at every update, so its effect on mean survivor accuracy can be smaller than, or differently signed from, its effect on its own frequency. This is why the two outcomes were assigned separate estimands, scales and decision rules in advance.

### 2.8 Study 002: separating directional cache information from displacement

Study 002 retains every Study 001 operator: the population, genotype, mutation rate 1/32, cache inheritance, four-candidate budget with 128 objective queries per update, exact integer-weighted survival, 256-update horizon and late window. It uses only the HALF law, which remains deliberately aligned to the cache delay; there is no ZERO arm. It changes only the policy probe, in three arms. For a parent with genotype `x` and valid cache `c`:

- **INFO:** a valid `M` carrier proposes `c`, exactly as in the Study 001 ACTIVE arm;
- **NONINFO:** a valid `M` carrier proposes `c` at innovation updates (t < 3 or `R_t = 0`), and at recurrent updates (t >= 3 and `R_t = 1`) proposes `x XOR pi_(b,t)(c XOR x)`, the cache displacement with its bit positions moved by a shared permutation; and
- **SHAM:** every label uses the fresh construction, as in Study 001.

`F` carriers and invalid-cache `M` carriers use the fresh construction in every arm. The experimental operator reads `R_t`; the evolving policy cannot. INFO and NONINFO therefore follow identical rules at innovation updates and differ only in whether a valid cache displacement keeps its direction at recurrent updates. Both pay the same one-proposal displacement cost at every valid-`M` use.

`pi_(b,t)` is one exact uniform random permutation of the 32 bit positions, generated for every block b and update t regardless of branch or arm by a Fisher-Yates shuffle with unbiased multiply-high rejection, and shared by all cells and all parents of that block and update (Supplementary Section S18). Because permuting bit positions preserves Hamming weight, a NONINFO decoy lies exactly as far from its parent as the cache does. The shared permutation also preserves overlaps among the displacement masks of different parents under one common relabeling of coordinates, but it does not preserve every cross-genotype relation; for example, siblings that share one cached genotype do not share one decoy endpoint. A displacement of weight 0 or 32 cannot be scrambled and retains its direction; these cases are counted in the diagnostics.

Each of 41,600 new independent blocks contains six paired cells, arm (INFO, NONINFO, SHAM) x policy start (ALL_F, ALL_M), sharing 32 initial uniform genotypes and every keyed random value at compatible coordinates, including the permutations. Random values come from the same Philox4x32-10 construction under a new namespace; no genotype, target, trajectory, block, random tape or outcome from Study 001 was reused. The Study 002 producer extends the authenticated Study 001 producer, and its unchanged operators were required to remain fixture-identical. SHAM obeys the same identities N1 and N2 as in Study 001, so its start-averaged late-window memory-use frequency is exactly 1/2 in every block; it serves both as the neutral allele benchmark and as the no-memory performance reference.

For block b, let `P(arm, start)` be the late-window mean population accuracy and `L(arm, start)` the late-window mean memory-use frequency, and let `P_arm` and `A_arm` be their equal-weight averages over the two starts. The primary family contains three estimands:

- `Delta_P = P_INFO - P_NONINFO`, the total population-accuracy effect of directional cache information beyond the parent and matched displacement length, mediated through evolved policy use; and
- `D_INFO = L(INFO, ALL_M) - L(INFO, ALL_F)` and `D_NONINFO = L(NONINFO, ALL_M) - L(NONINFO, ALL_F)`, the finite-horizon start-state gate.

INFO and NONINFO share policy rules but not realized policy states, so `Delta_P` includes any divergence in policy frequency, cache use, survivor composition and descendants. It is a total effect, not a per-use value-of-information estimate. Two secondary families cannot alter the primary decision. The allele family contains `E_INFO = A_INFO - A_NONINFO`, the allele-frequency effect of cache direction, and `E_NONINFO = A_NONINFO - 1/2`, selection under the matched control relative to exact SHAM neutrality. The no-memory performance family contains `B_INFO = P_INFO - P_SHAM` and `B_NONINFO = P_NONINFO - P_SHAM`.

The meaningful scale is again 1/32: one correct bit in mean accuracy (3.125 percentage points) or one expected individual in allele frequency. Intervals are two-sided Hoeffding-Bonferroni intervals over n = 41,600 blocks. The primary family uses alpha_each = 0.05/3; each primary block variable has range length 2, giving half-width 0.01517128, below Delta/2 = 0.015625. Each secondary family uses alpha_each = 0.025; `E_INFO`, `B_INFO` and `B_NONINFO` have range length 2 and half-width 0.01451463, and `E_NONINFO` has range length 1 and half-width 0.00725731. The three Study 002 families are controlled separately from one another and from the Study 001 families.

The frozen primary decision order was: (1) failure of any deterministic identity, coordinate, query-count, saved-output or audit check gives INVALID; (2) if either gate interval is not wholly inside (-1/32, +1/32), the result is START-DEPENDENT and the primary unresolved; (3) a lower bound of `Delta_P` above 1/32 gives a meaningful positive directional-information effect relative to NONINFO; (4) an upper bound below -1/32 gives a meaningful adverse effect; (5) an upper bound at or below 1/32 gives BOUNDED BELOW THE POSITIVE ONE-BIT SCALE, with a separate statement of whether the interval lies wholly inside (-1/32, +1/32); (6) otherwise UNRESOLVED. Each secondary estimand was classified against the same scale. Even a positive primary would have established a benefit relative to no-memory use only if the lower bound of `B_INFO` also exceeded 1/32. No sample extension, parameter change or follow-up cohort follows any branch.

### 2.9 Study 003: the same question in an alternative operator bundle

Study 003 asks whether the Study 001 classification, recurrence-attributable enrichment of a mutation-generated private-cache-use allele under a one-proposal opportunity cost, recurs in one independently implemented operator bundle that changes the alphabet, the loss, the proposal semantics and the survival rule together. It keeps every element that defines the question: 32 haploid individuals; the supplied one-record lineage cache; the `M`/`F` policy allele, which couples retention and retrieval; symmetric policy mutation at rate 1/32; four evaluated candidates per parent and 128 objective queries per update; replacement of exactly one fresh proposal by the cache; the ZERO and HALF target laws, with HALF still deliberately aligned to the cache delay; the 256-update horizon and late window; and the eight paired cells, ACTIVE or SHAM x ZERO or HALF x ALL_F or ALL_M, in each of 41,600 new independent blocks.

Each phenotype is a vector of 32 circular loci, each an unsigned 16-bit integer in Z/2^16 Z. For locus i with `u = (x_i - t_i) mod 2^16`, the circular distance is `d_i = min(u, 2^16 - u)`, and individual loss is the exact integer `Q = sum_i d_i^2`, so loss grows gradually with distance on the torus. The policy probe of an `F` carrier, of an invalid-cache `M` carrier and of every label in SHAM is an independent uniform 32-coordinate fresh vector; a valid ACTIVE-`M` carrier proposes its cached phenotype instead. The global scout is a second independent uniform vector. The local child takes the smallest-loss member of the first three candidates and independently replaces each coordinate, with probability 1/32, by a uniform 16-bit value. This is large-alphabet resampling, not a small torus step, and the fresh proposals are uniform rather than local: torus geometry enters through graded loss, not through proposal generation.

Survival uses rank only. For each of the 32 survivor slots, four entries are drawn independently, with replacement, from the 128 candidates; the entry with the lowest exact loss wins, ties being broken by a label-blind keyed 64-bit candidate tie key and then by candidate index. A candidate can win zero, one or several slots. Tournament size four was frozen because 32 slots x four entries equals the 128-candidate pool; it was not calibrated to the survival strength of Study 001. The unique best candidate has expected offspring 8290815/8388608, just below one, and leaves no descendant with probability above one-third, so survival is much weaker than near-deterministic preservation of a clearly best candidate. Each survivor inherits its producing parent's policy, flips `M <-> F` with probability 1/32 and, if `M` after mutation, caches its producing parent's pre-update phenotype; there is no phenotype mutation after survival. No label, cache or lineage enters ranking or tie-breaking. Population accuracy is the exact rational `(2^40 - total survivor loss)/2^40`.

The entire implementation, including canonical Random123 Threefry4x64-20 addressing keyed by purpose with counters (block, update, entity, subindex), was written anew from the frozen specification without importing or adapting producer, analyzer or auditor code from Studies 001 or 002, and every phenotype, target, random tape and cohort is new. SHAM never reads a label or cache when constructing phenotypes, so identities N1 and N2 and the neutral expectations of Section 2.5 hold unchanged.

The estimands `C_abs`, `C_rec`, `D_HALF`, `D_ZERO`, `P_abs` and `P_rec`, their range lengths and the two Hoeffding-Bonferroni families (alpha_each = 0.05/4 and 0.05/2 over n = 41,600 blocks) are those of Section 2.6, so the half-widths are again 0.00781023, 0.01562046, 0.01451463 and 0.02902925. The allele scale is again Delta = 1/32, one expected individual. The performance scale Delta_P = 1/32 is 1/32 of the maximal total loss: on average, moving one coordinate per individual from the torus antipode to exact agreement, or removing roughly three typical random-coordinate losses per individual. It is not a correct bit, so Study 003 effect magnitudes are not compared numerically with those of Studies 001 or 002; only the prespecified within-bundle classifications are compared. The primary decision order is that of Section 2.6, with rule 3 reading SUPPORTS RECURRENCE-ATTRIBUTABLE SELECTIVE ENRICHMENT IN THIS ALTERNATIVE OPERATOR BUNDLE; only rule 3 reproduces the Study 001 classification, and a start-dependent or unresolved result would have been no answer rather than evidence against the mechanism. The performance classification order is that of Section 2.6 and cannot alter the primary decision. A crossed interpretation was also frozen: primary support with `P_abs` bounded below the positive scale would mean that the selection/performance separation recurs in this operator bundle, and primary support with a meaningfully positive `P_abs` would mean that the separation is bundle-specific at the declared scales. No branch permitted added cohorts, another tournament, another operator class or a component-isolating follow-up. Supplementary Section S27 compares the two operator bundles element by element.

### 3.15 The three-study result across two operator bundles

The three prospective experiments give one paper-level result in two operator bundles. In the bit/Hamming bundle, recurrence strongly selected a costly memory-use policy (Study 001), and directional cache information strongly amplified that selection (Study 002), without a resolved one-bit gain in population accuracy. In one independently implemented torus/tournament bundle with new cohorts (Study 003), the frozen primary classification of Study 001 recurred, the recurrent-law ACTIVE-SHAM performance contrast was again bounded below the positive 1/32 scale with sign unresolved, and the recurrence interaction in performance was again unresolved; the frozen crossed interpretation is that the selection/performance separation recurs in this operator bundle. This is agreement between prespecified within-bundle classifications. Because alphabet, loss, proposal and resampling semantics, and survival all differ between the bundles, Study 003 shows that the separation is not confined to the original bundle, but it does not show that the separation holds in every bundle, and it cannot say which change, if any, matters.

The 22 Study 001, 19 Study 002 and 22 Study 003 records are three separately accepted estimate sets, 63 saved estimate records in total, each with its own familywise families; none is pooled with another. Effect magnitudes are not compared across bundles, because the performance scale has a different meaning in Study 003 and its accuracy levels sit on a different baseline.

## 4. Discussion

The central observation is a separation between what selection favored and what the population gained. In Study 001, under the aligned recurrent law, more than four-fifths of late-window survivors carried the memory-use allele in both start conditions over the 256-update horizon, whereas the ACTIVE-SHAM accuracy contrast, in which SHAM labels are inert, was bounded below one additional correct bit per survivor. Under the nonrecurrent law, where the cache carried no lag-two information, the allele fell descriptively below its neutral frequency, and ACTIVE accuracy was descriptively slightly below SHAM.

Study 001 was not designed to identify the mechanism of this separation, and the following explanations are hypotheses for future work rather than findings. Survival depends on the relative scores of candidates within an update. A carrier whose cache matches a recurrent target supplies a candidate that outscores most fresh proposals, so carriers can be strongly overrepresented among survivors even if the survivor set, which is dominated by the best candidates from all families, improves only modestly on average. At non-recurrent updates the same carriers have given up one fresh draw, and the local child of every parent already supplies a near-copy of the best current candidate, which may limit what a retrieved record adds to the population's mean fit. The ZERO accuracy contrast is in the direction expected from displaced exploration, but it is small and descriptive.

Study 002 sharpens this separation rather than reversing it. Its control removes the direction of the retrieved displacement at recurrent updates while keeping the same displacement cost and the same distance from a parent that has just survived selection. Relative to that control, directional information added a large and well-resolved selective advantage to the memory-use policy, but the total population-accuracy effect of the same information was bounded between -0.652 and +2.382 percentage points. Two further observations are descriptive and motivate hypotheses only. First, NONINFO was itself selected well above neutrality; one possibility is that a candidate placed at the cache's distance from a recently selected parent tends to score better than a uniform fresh proposal even without directional information, particularly because a large share of displacements had weight 0 and so reproduced the parent (Supplementary Section S23). Second, neither arm showed a resolved accuracy gain over SHAM, consistent with the Study 001 mechanisms suggested above, in which strong relative advantage among candidates can coexist with a small change in the survivors' mean fit. Study 002 was not designed to test these explanations.

Study 003 asks whether the separation belongs only to the original operator bundle. Its bundle replaces the binary alphabet with 2^16 circular alleles per locus, Hamming mismatch with graded squared circular loss, XOR-mask proposals and bit flips with uniform vectors and coordinate resampling, and exact weighted sampling without replacement with rank-only tournaments in which even the unique best candidate leaves no descendant with probability above one-third; it was implemented anew with new cohorts. Recurrence-attributable enrichment of the same supplied-cache-use policy recurred, and the recurrent-law performance contrast was again bounded below the declared positive scale with sign unresolved. The selection/performance separation is therefore not specific to Hamming loss or to near-deterministic preservation of the best candidate, but Study 003 was not designed to test the mechanisms suggested above, and because all of its operators changed together it cannot attribute either the recurrence of the classification or any difference in magnitude to a particular component.

These results are consistent in spirit with prior findings that the marginal benefit of learning or memory can be small even where memory is favored [4], and with models in which memory-related loci or anticipatory effects evolve only under particular environmental regimes [5, 7]. The present design adds an exact neutral benchmark for a mutable use allele, a cost expressed in the same currency as search itself, and prospectively separate scales for enrichment and performance. Its main methodological implication is that an allele-frequency response to environmental structure, however strong, should not be read as a population-performance response without a separately stated performance estimand and scale. Study 002 extends that implication to the information content of the retrieved record itself: even an allele-frequency effect attributable to directional information, measured against a control matched on displacement cost and parent distance, need not convert into a commensurate population-performance effect. Study 003 shows that the implication is not tied to one operator bundle.

The result is deliberately narrow. HALF matches the recurrence lag to the retrieval delay and so favors retrieval by construction; no other lag or copy probability was tested in either bundle. The opportunity cost is specific to replacing one effectively uniform proposal in a four-candidate budget. The cache architecture was supplied rather than evolved. The Study 002 control matches parent distance and one common coordinate relabeling, not every relation among genotypes, and its primary estimand is a total effect through evolved policy use rather than a per-use value of information. Study 003 adds one alternative operator bundle, not a family of bundles, and its proposals are uniform rather than gradual. Within those conditions, the allele inferences are strong and the performance inferences are bounded or unresolved; none should be generalized beyond them.

## 5. Limitations

- **One aligned recurrent law.** HALF was a prior, outcome-informed choice that matches the cache delay by construction, and Study 002 used HALF alone. Study 003 used the same ZERO and HALF laws in its own bundle. No other lag, copy probability or target law was tested, and the result does not establish selection under arbitrary recurrence.
- **One fixed cost per bundle.** The cost is the replacement of one of two effectively uniform fresh proposals. It is a model-specific search-budget trade-off, not a calibrated energetic or biological cost and not a general cost scale. No other cost was tested in either bundle.
- **One mutation rate and one horizon; two operator bundles.** All three studies use mutation rate 1/32 and 256 updates. Studies 001 and 002 share one abstract model class; Study 002 extends the Study 001 producer and is not an independent model-class reproduction. Study 003 adds one independently implemented alternative operator bundle, which is neither a sample from nor a test of operator bundles in general.
- **One alternative bundle; no component attribution.** Study 003 changed the alphabet, the loss, the proposal and resampling semantics and the survival rule together. Its result cannot be attributed to any one of them, and it does not establish universal replication, continuous or gradual search (its proposals are uniform and its local operator resamples coordinates rather than taking small steps), or the outcome under any other tournament size.
- **Supplied architecture.** The one-record private cache and its retrieval rule were supplied. Only the use policy mutated. No study addresses the spontaneous origin of memory architecture or capacity.
- **Retention and retrieval are not separated.** In all three studies the allele jointly determines whether the one-step record is retained and whether it is retrieved; every individual has a cache slot, but only `M` carriers receive a valid record, so this design cannot separate selection on retention from selection on retrieval.
- **A total, not per-use, information effect.** `Delta_P` is the total population-accuracy effect of directional cache information beyond the parent and matched displacement length, mediated through evolved policy use. INFO and NONINFO diverge in policy frequency, cache use and survivors, so `Delta_P` is not a per-use value-of-information estimate and supports no per-use causal claim.
- **Control geometry.** The shared permutation preserves each decoy's distance to its parent and one common coordinate relabeling of displacement masks, but not every cross-genotype relation. Displacements of weight 0 or 32 cannot be scrambled and retain direction. In NONINFO, 42,175,734 of 100,158,252 decoy applications from ALL_F and 45,696,359 of 108,736,742 from ALL_M had weight 0 (42.1% and 42.0%); none had weight 32.
- **Finite horizon; start-state gate only.** In all three studies, both late-window start contrasts were bounded within one expected individual (95% familywise) over 256 updates. This is not a demonstration of general start-independence, stationarity, equilibrium, evolutionary stability, rare-mutant invasion or fixation probability.
- **Performance not resolved at the one-bit scale.** `P_abs` excludes a gain of one correct bit but does not resolve its sign; `P_rec` is unresolved, and its half-width is close to the one-bit scale. In Study 002, `Delta_P`, `B_INFO` and `B_NONINFO` exclude a positive one-bit effect but none resolves its sign. In Study 003, `P_abs` excludes a positive effect at its own 1/32 scale without resolving its sign, and `P_rec` is unresolved. Population benefit was not demonstrated in any study. The small adverse ZERO accuracy contrasts and the ZERO allele deficits in Studies 001 and 003 are descriptive.
- **Separate families and separate estimate sets.** Each of the two Study 001 families, the three Study 002 families and the two Study 003 families has 95% familywise coverage; coverage is not controlled jointly across families, across studies or across the 44 descriptive means. The 22 Study 001, 19 Study 002 and 22 Study 003 records are three separately accepted estimate sets and are not pooled or presented as independent replications.
- **What SHAM does not control.** SHAM is an exact neutral benchmark for label dynamics, not a control for alternative caches, alternative costs or alternative retrieval rules. ACTIVE and SHAM genotypes and genealogies diverge at the first valid-M retrieval, so ACTIVE-SHAM contrasts are arm-level comparisons, not path-level counterfactuals; the same holds for INFO, NONINFO and SHAM in Study 002 and for ACTIVE and SHAM in Study 003.
- **Abstract model.** The genotype, objective, selection rule and population are abstract. No biological, ecological, neural or engineering extrapolation and no claim of general population utility is supported, and the study does not replicate earlier internal private-memory studies.
- **Internal audit only.** Replay and record verification were performed by separately written tools within the project, partly with AI assistance. They are not external peer review or an independent scientific replication. Producer and audit tools were both written with AI assistance from the same specification; the audits detect implementation divergence, not a shared misreading of the specification. In Study 003 the first audit attempt stopped at three malformed audit-file headers; the recovery changed 12 header bytes in a derived audit tree only, and the original files and the failed attempt are preserved.
- **No follow-up variants.** No study's specification permits a numerical extension, parameter grid, cost or rate sweep, lag variant, alternative tournament, new cohort or top-up in response to its outcome, and none is proposed here.
- **Bounded literature search.** The literature comparison rests on a structured search of open scholarly metadata with targeted citation chaining, not an exhaustive systematic review; it supports a bounded distinct-contribution claim only and cannot establish a unique nearest precedent. The search archive will be included in the public package (Section 7).

## 6. Methods summary

For Study 001, the model, cohorts and analysis follow the frozen specification PHASE2-MUTABLE-MEMORY-001 revision 1 exactly; Supplementary Sections S2-S10 give the full update order, target laws, allele mutation, cache inheritance and use, candidate budget, selection, estimands, interval construction and decision rules. Random values were drawn from canonical Random123 Philox4x32-10 streams whose keys are derived from SHA-256 of a purpose-specific namespace string and whose counters are the coordinates (block, update, entity, subindex); a coordinate-schema enumeration and collision audit preceded production. Ten deterministic fixtures, including hand traces, memory-better and memory-worse cases, duplicate and invalid-cache cases, full SHAM identity paths, target-law pairing, an exact weighted-selection fixture with a forced rejection, random-coordinate invariance and single-path regeneration, passed before production; none contains a sampled scientific outcome.

Per-update binary records were saved for every path, per-path late-window summaries and final-state hashes for every path, and full candidate-level rows for the fixed audit blocks 0-63. The frozen analyzer computed the 22 estimate records, their bounds and classifications from saved path outputs. Interval endpoints use exact rational estimates and high-precision half-widths. Descriptive diagnostics (mutation counts, cache-probe use and survival, and occupancy counters) were saved for interpretation only (Supplementary Section S12).

Study 002 follows the frozen specification PHASE2-PERFORMANCE-CONVERSION-002 revision 1 exactly; Supplementary Sections S16-S26 give the arm definitions, shared-permutation construction, event order, estimands, interval families, frozen decision order, all 19 records, diagnostics and audit. Random values come from the same Philox construction under the new namespace `PHASE2-PERFORMANCE-CONVERSION-002|production-r1|<purpose>`, with one added purpose, `DECOY_PERMUTATION`, generated for every block and update regardless of branch or arm. The specification required ten deterministic fixtures before production, covering bit-identity of the unchanged operators, Fisher-Yates and rejection fixtures with forced retries, INFO-NONINFO identity through innovation updates, distance matching at all 33 displacement weights, preservation of weights and overlaps under the shared permutation, the uniform subset law of the decoy, full SHAM identity paths, random-coordinate invariance and single-path regeneration; none contains a sampled production outcome. Per-update records, per-path summaries and final hashes, the 19 estimate records and full candidate rows and permutations for audit blocks 0-63 were saved. The frozen analyzer computed all estimates from saved path outputs in exact rational arithmetic with high-precision half-widths.

Study 003 follows the frozen specification PHASE2-TORUS-MEMORY-003 revision 1 exactly; Supplementary Sections S27-S39 give the exact torus loss, proposal construction, tournament and tie semantics, event order, Threefry key, counter and lane addressing, SHAM identities, estimands, interval families, decision order, all 22 records, execution and audit provenance and exact claim limits. Random values come from canonical Random123 Threefry4x64-20, pinned to one official reference commit whose known-answer vectors both producer and auditor were required to pass. Each purpose's four 64-bit key words are taken from SHA-256 of `PHASE2-TORUS-MEMORY-003|production-r1|<PURPOSE>`, every counter is (block, update, entity, subindex), and each 32-coordinate vector is read from two calls as sixteen 16-bit lanes per call. The specification required sixteen deterministic fixtures before production, including the Threefry known-answer vectors, a complete address enumeration and collision audit, circular-distance wrap and antipode cases, maximal-loss cases, one- and two-update hand traces, donor, tournament and candidate-key tie cases, the exact 128-rank tournament table, full SHAM paths and single-path regeneration; none samples a scientific block. Per-update records, per-path summaries and final hashes, per-block variables with N1/N2 flags, full candidate, tournament-entry and context rows for audit blocks 0-63 and the 22 estimate records were saved. The frozen analyzer computed every estimate in exact rational arithmetic from saved block numerators, with high-precision half-widths.

This is an abstract computational model. It involves no human participants, no animals and no personal data.
